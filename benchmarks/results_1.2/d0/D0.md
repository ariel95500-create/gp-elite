## How the typed start works in 1.1.0

Under `units=`, each of the three islands (133 places) is filled by
`dim_search.typed_random_tree`.

1. It first tries a fast path, `_monomial_tree`. This step solves by least
   squares the integer exponents e_i that make the product of the input
   units equal to the target unit. It then builds that product with a free
   coefficient. It accepts the product only when the sum of the |e_i| is at
   most 8 and each |e_i| at most 4.
2. Otherwise it runs a blind search, `_build`. This search builds random
   trees of the target unit, at depth 2 to 7. It makes up to 40 attempts,
   each of at most 4,000 steps.

A draw that fails returns None. `Island.initialize` then draws again, until
the island is full or 20 draws per place have failed, and completes the
island with copies of what it has. Nothing in this looks at the deadline.

## 1. Where the time of III.19.51 goes

The target is an energy, m·q⁴/(ε²·h²) with n dimensionless. Its exponents
are (1, 4, −2, −2), and their sum is 9. The fast path refuses this product
and never gives a tree.

- **Duration.** The start takes 443.5 s of a fit of 444.6 s, and the fit then
  runs one generation. On the first machine of the prototypes this was 600 to
  735 s.
- **Draws.** It takes 3,549 typed draws to fill 399 places. 3,150 of them
  return None (89 %), and they take 417.6 s of the 443.5 s (94 %). The
  islands end full of real trees: no copy is needed, at about 1.1 s per tree.
- **The blind search.** It is called 134,022 times. 111,203 of those calls
  run out of their 4,000 steps (83 %), and 399 build a tree (0.3 %). In all,
  that is 459 million steps of `_build_ek`.

The start has no other fault: the time is not lost in a loop, a repeated
computation or a numerical failure. It is the blind search, used where it
almost never succeeds, with a cost that has no bound in time: up to 20 × 133
failed draws per island, each of up to 160,000 steps.

## 2. III.19.51 is alone

In all the other 80 fits the start takes at most 0.56 s.

- **The spring.** Under units it is the slowest of these. Its √(m/k) has
  half exponents, so it goes through the blind search, which succeeds there.
- **The products in U41.** Of the 41 equations of U41, 30 ask for an exact
  product of integer powers (`d0_dims.json`). Their sums of exponents go from
  1 to 9. I.32.5 sits at the limit (8). Only III.19.51 is above it.

## 3. Why the draws are hard, and what the slow start brings

Nine factors, four of them the same charge, are rarely reached by random
trees of depth at most 7 in 4,000 steps. Those trees reach it through
squares (`(v1²)²`).

The slow start is also what returns the law. In the campaigns of 1.1
(`../../results_1.1/campaign/U41_*.jsonl`), III.19.51 under units is exact at
all ten seeds and near under noise, after a single generation: trees of the
start already have the structure of the law, n² included
(`-0.00316629 * v0 * (v1²)² / (v2² * v3² * v4²)`).

## What this means for the correction (b) as written

Correction (b) as written stops the start at the deadline and completes the
population with copies. On this machine, after 30 s, the first island would
hold about 27 trees and the other two none. `Island.initialize` raises an
error when an island has no tree (`core.py` of 1.1.0, lines 6894 to 6904).
Even with one tree per island guaranteed, the start would no longer hold the
trees that carry the law. That is not measured.
