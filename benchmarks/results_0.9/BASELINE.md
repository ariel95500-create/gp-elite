# Phase 1 — baseline of gp-elite 0.8.0, frozen test set, reproducibility

Measured on 5 October 2026 by `benchmarks/phase1_bench.py` with the plan of
`PLAN.md` (committed at 09:17, before the first fit at 09:26). Raw records:
`baseline_0.8.0.jsonl` (1,775 fits, SHA-256 `b223d570…3b3ccc00`); tables
regenerated by `python benchmarks/phase1_report.py
benchmarks/results_0.9/baseline_0.8.0.jsonl`.

Engine: gp-elite 0.8.0 installed from PyPI in a fresh environment (every
record names the engine file it imported). Machine: 4 cores (Intel Xeon at
2.1 GHz with AVX-512), Python 3.11.15, NumPy 2.4.6, scikit-learn 1.9.1, three
fits at a time, one process per fit, `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, one BLAS thread.
Budget T: 30 s per fit. No fit crashed. The machine of the session restarted
once, at about 09:33, after 77 fits: the fits running at that moment left no
record and were run again in full when the measurement resumed.

## What the numbers say

- **F41 on this machine: 88 exact laws of 205, the same 88 (equation and
  seed) as campaign R of 0.8**, although the machine and the load differ
  (median generations in 30 s: 70 here, 76 in campaign R).
- **The five families at zero stay at zero:** roots, trigonometry, rational
  forms, exponentials and logarithms, 18 equations, 0 exact in 90 fits.
- **Frozen test set, Feynman (TF78): 145 exact of 390.** The 58 main
  equations do better than F41 (144 of 290, 50 % against 43 %); the 20 bonus
  equations almost never (1 of 100). 32 of the 78 equations are found at
  least once, 25 at all five seeds.
- **Strogatz (TS14): 8 exact of 70** on random splits and 5 of 70 out of
  domain (4 systems of 14 found at least once), with 27 collapses of 70 out of
  domain.
- **Noise:** no exact law with 1 % or 10 % noise, as expected (the criterion
  is precision to 1e-9); the structure of the law is retrieved in 70 fits of
  205 at 1 % and 67 at 10 % (88 without noise).
- **Real data of the decision bench** as in 0.8: R6 median test R² 0.809 over
  folds; out of domain 4 collapses of 30 on R6 and 6 of 35 on R7raw (10 of
  65), the worst at R² −224 and −233. Formulas that do not reproduce the
  model: 0 of 60 on R6, 8 of 70 on R7raw (122 of 130 reproduce it).
- **Real data of the test set (TR25) collapse far more often:** out of
  domain 37 of 125 standardised and 27 of 125 in their own units, and even 10
  and 8 of 125 within the folds; standardised, the worst reach R² −1.7e6
  (folds) and −1.7e12 (out of domain). The collapses concentrate on small or mostly categorical
  datasets (`solar_flare`, `557_analcatdata_apnea1`, `665_sleuth_case2002`,
  `1096_FacultySalaries`, `first_principles_planck`, …). The six datasets of
  the decision bench understated this risk.
- **Formulas flagged inexact** (a numerical safety net acts): 6 of 205 on
  F41, 14 of 390 on TF78, 19 of 125 folds on TR25.

## Reproducibility from one processor to another

The thirteen reference configurations of `benchmarks/speed_equivalence.py`
with the 0.8.0 wheel, compared field by field (`repro/compare_*.txt`):

| comparison | configurations returning a different model |
|---|---|
| default, run twice | 0 of 13 |
| without AVX-512, run twice | 0 of 13 |
| default against without AVX-512 | 9 of 13 |
| default against without AVX-512 nor AVX2 | 9 of 13 |
| without AVX-512 against without AVX-512 nor AVX2 | 5 of 13 (one in the Pareto front only) |

On one machine a fit is reproduced exactly, run after run. Across processors
with different instruction sets, most fits return a different model at equal
seed: NumPy computes `exp`, `tanh`... with the instructions available, the
last digit changes and the search takes another path. The five that do not
change are `A_feyn`, `D_units` and `K_thorough` (the same Feynman data),
`F_robust` and `H_parallel`. This is what the README now says (pull request
#2).

## Not measured in this run

The units mode (Feynman II.11.3, 149 s per run in 0.8) is measured with
`benchmarks/ab_ood.py`; it is measured again on this machine at the start of
phase 3, whose subject it is.
